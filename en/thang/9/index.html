Where to go in Vietnam in September? 13 best destinations

Golden rice season in Mu Cang Chai and Hoang Su Phi; the 2 September holiday is busy; rains reach central Vietnam late in the month.

Northern Vietnam

  • Ha Long Bay (Quang Ninh) – A natural wonder of the world in the Gulf of Tonkin. Best time: April – October.
  • Sa Pa (Lao Cai) – A town in the mist and its terraced rice fields. Best time: September – November (rice harvest).
  • Ban Gioc Waterfall (Cao Bang) – Southeast Asia's most beautiful border waterfall. Best time: August – October (high water, ripe rice).
  • Mu Cang Chai (Lao Cai (formerly Yen Bai)) – The most beautiful rice terraces of the Northwest. Best time: September – October (ripe rice), May – June (flooded fields).
  • Hanoi (Capital city) – A thousand years of culture around Hoan Kiem Lake. Best time: September – November (autumn).
  • Cat Ba – Lan Ha Bay (Hai Phong) – A green island beside unspoilt Lan Ha Bay. Best time: April – September.
  • Co To Island (Quang Ninh) – Unspoilt blue seas at the northeastern frontier. Best time: April – September.
  • Mai Chau Valley (Phu Tho (formerly Hoa Binh)) – Peaceful Thai villages in a valley of rice. Best time: March – May, September – October.

Central Vietnam

  • Quy Nhon (Gia Lai (formerly Binh Dinh)) – A peaceful seaside city in the land of martial arts. Best time: March – September.
  • Pu Luong (Thanh Hoa) – Rice terraces, water wheels and peaceful Thai villages. Best time: May – June, September – October.

Southern Vietnam

  • Con Dao (Ho Chi Minh City (formerly Ba Ria – Vung Tau)) – Unspoilt nature and moving history. Best time: March – September.
  • Can Tho – Mekong Delta (Can Tho) – Floating markets, fruit orchards and river life. Best time: September – November (flood season).
  • Chau Doc – Sam Mountain (An Giang) – A spiritual land and cajuput forests in the flood season. Best time: September – November (flood season).

Festivals & things to know in September

  • Full-moon night in the old town · Hoi An Ancient Town Monthly · lunar calendar

    Every 14th lunar evening the old town switches to lantern light only.

    Check the lunar calendar to catch the full-moon night.

  • Sea turtle nesting season · Con Dao May – September

    Watch turtles nest and hatchlings released on Bay Canh island.

    Must be booked in advance with Con Dao National Park.

  • Rough-sea season · Phu Quoc, Con Dao, Ha Tien, Cat Ba – Lan Ha Bay June – September

    Monsoon winds bring rough seas; island tours and ferries may be cancelled.

    Keep a spare day in case boats are cancelled.

  • National Day holiday (2 Sep) 30/08 – 03/09

    An end-of-summer break with celebrations in Hanoi and other cities.

    Room prices rise – book at least 2–3 weeks ahead.

  • Mid-Autumn Festival · Hanoi, Hoi An Ancient Town September – October · lunar calendar

    15th of the 8th lunar month: lantern-filled Hang Ma street and Hoi An, lion dances.

    Evenings are packed – park further out and walk in.

  • Golden rice terraces · Mu Cang Chai, Sa Pa, Ha Giang, Pu Luong 05/09 – 15/10

    Terraces turn gold – best from late September to early October.

    Weekends are crowded and homestays fill up fast.

  • Mekong flood season · Can Tho – Mekong Delta, Chau Doc – Sam Mountain September – November

    Rising water brings linh fish, sesbania flowers and lush Tra Su forest.

    Try linh fish hotpot – only available this season.

  • Central Vietnam storm season · Hue Imperial City, Da Nang, Hoi An Ancient Town, Phong Nha – Ke Bang, Quy Nhon, Phu Yen – Ganh Da Dia, Ly Son Island, Nha Trang September – November

    Heavy rain, possible floods and typhoons; Hoi An and Hue may flood.

    Watch forecasts and book flexible tickets/rooms; island boats may be suspended.

  • Cham Kate Festival · Ninh Thuan – Vinh Hy September – October · lunar calendar

    The seventh month of the Cham calendar (usually September–October) – costume processions, music and dance at the Po Klong Garai and Po Rome towers.

    Check the official dates first; respect the rites and don't cut into the procession.

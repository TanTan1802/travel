Where to go in Vietnam in January? 15 best destinations

The north is cold and dry, with possible frost in the mountains; the south is in its sunny dry season. Tết usually falls in late January or February.

Northern Vietnam

  • Moc Chau Plateau (Son La) – Emerald tea hills and white mustard blossoms. Best time: October – March.

Central Vietnam

  • Hue Imperial City (Hue City) – The Nguyen dynasty citadel beside the dreamy Perfume River. Best time: January – April.
  • Da Lat (Lam Dong) – The city of a thousand flowers on the Lang Biang plateau. Best time: November – March (flower season).
  • Nha Trang (Khanh Hoa) – The pearl of the East Sea. Best time: January – August.
  • Mui Ne (Lam Dong (formerly Binh Thuan)) – Vietnam's "little Sahara". Best time: November – April.
  • Buon Ma Thuot (Dak Lak) – Vietnam's coffee capital and the Central Highlands. Best time: November – March.
  • Ninh Thuan – Vinh Hy (Khanh Hoa (formerly Ninh Thuan)) – Sun, wind, vineyards and ancient Cham towers. Best time: January – August.
  • Mang Den (Quang Ngai (formerly Kon Tum)) – A "second Da Lat" in the Central Highlands forest. Best time: November – April.

Southern Vietnam

  • Phu Quoc (An Giang (formerly Kien Giang)) – The pearl island with Vietnam's most beautiful sunsets. Best time: November – April.
  • Ho Chi Minh City (Saigon) – A vibrant city that never sleeps. Best time: December – April (dry season).
  • Vung Tau (Ho Chi Minh City (formerly Ba Ria – Vung Tau)) – The closest beach city to Saigon. Best time: November – April.
  • Ha Tien (An Giang (formerly Kien Giang)) – A gentle border town on the Gulf of Thailand. Best time: November – April.
  • Tay Ninh – Ba Den Mountain (Tay Ninh) – The roof of the south and the Cao Dai Holy See. Best time: December – April.
  • Ca Mau Cape (Ca Mau) – The southernmost tip, where mangroves reach into the sea. Best time: December – April.
  • Nam Du Islands (An Giang (formerly Kien Giang)) – Unspoilt islands in the Gulf of Thailand. Best time: November – April.

Festivals & things to know in January

  • Lunar New Year (Tết) January – February · lunar calendar

    The biggest holiday of the year: flower markets, busy temples and a festive mood.

    Many shops close for 3–5 days; transport and rooms get much pricier – book 1–2 months ahead.

  • Full-moon night in the old town · Hoi An Ancient Town Monthly · lunar calendar

    Every 14th lunar evening the old town switches to lantern light only.

    Check the lunar calendar to catch the full-moon night.

  • Plum and peach blossom season · Moc Chau Plateau January – February

    Na Ka valley turns white with plum blossom, dotted with pink peach.

    It is cold and misty – bring warm layers.

  • Cherry blossom season · Da Lat January – February

    Pink cherry blossoms around Xuan Huong Lake and Cau Dat tea hills.

    Often overlaps with Tết – book early.

  • Ba Den Mountain Spring Festival · Tay Ninh – Ba Den Mountain January – March · lunar calendar

    From Tet to the end of the first lunar month – the busiest pilgrimage season of the year.

    Buy cable car tickets ahead, go very early and avoid the full-moon day.

  • Nguyen Hue Tet flower street · Ho Chi Minh City January – February · lunar calendar

    For about a week around Tết, Nguyen Hue walking street becomes a flower avenue with the zodiac mascot of the year and dazzling lights at night.

    Go early in the morning (6–8 am) for photos without crowds; evenings are packed.

  • H'Mong Gau Tao festival · Sa Pa, Ha Giang January – February · lunar calendar

    In early spring: a ceremonial pole, khen pipes, pao ball throwing, top spinning and horse racing – the biggest blessing festival of the H'Mong.

    Ask your homestay host which village holds it and when; ask before taking close-up photos of people.

  • Highland market days · Ha Giang, Sa Pa Monthly

    Rotating markets: Dong Van and Meo Vac (Ha Giang), Bac Ha and Can Cau (near Sa Pa) – colourful costumes, livestock trading, brocade and thang co stew.

    Most run on Sunday mornings (Can Cau: Saturday) and wind down by noon – plan to arrive by 7–8 am.

  • Cherry blossom in Mang Den · Mang Den January – February

    Cherry blossoms paint the town and pine-forest edges pink, often with a blossom festival and fair at the start of the year.

    Timing varies with the weather and lasts 1–2 weeks – follow local news before booking.

  • Kitesurfing season · Mui Ne November – March

    Steady north-east monsoon winds make Mui Ne one of Asia's best-known kitesurfing spots; the beach fills with colourful kites.

    Beginners should learn at a certified school (about 3 sessions); the sea is choppy and not ideal for small children.

  • Dry season – peak time on Phu Quoc · Phu Quoc, Ha Tien December – February

    Calm seas and clear skies – the most beautiful and busiest time.

    Book resorts, island tours and the cable car ahead.

  • Frost season – possible snow · Sa Pa December – January

    Fansipan and O Quy Ho may see frost or snow during strong cold spells.

    Temperatures can drop below 0°C – pack a down jacket, gloves and a beanie.
